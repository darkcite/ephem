Ephem

A chat that exists only while you are both here.

Direct, end-to-end encrypted conversations between two browsers. No account, no server, no history.

Start a chat

Free and open source. Runs entirely in your browser (WebAssembly).

How a chat starts

  1. You create an invite. Show it as a QR code, or send the link through any messenger.
  2. Your peer opens it and gets an answer code. They show or send it back to you.
  3. You apply the answer. The two browsers connect directly and agree on keys. Both screens show the same safety code.

Two codes are always needed, one each way: that is how browsers find a direct path to each other without a server in the middle. In person, this is two QR scans.

What Ephem does

  • Messages are encrypted in transit (DTLS) and end-to-end at the application layer (Noise).
  • Chat traffic travels directly between peers; the app uses no relay and rejects relay paths.
  • There is no server-side or app-side chat history. Close the tab and the chat is gone.
  • Your identity is a key, independent of your network connection.
  • Authenticity depends on how you exchanged codes. Compare the safety code when you did not meet in person.

What it does not do

  • In direct mode your peer can see your IP address, and so can the STUN servers used to find a path (Google, Cloudflare). A VPN can hide it; a built-in Tor mode is planned.
  • It cannot connect every pair of networks. If there is no direct path, it says so instead of silently using a relay.
  • It cannot deliver to someone who is offline, and it cannot stop a peer from copying what you send.
  • It cannot protect you from a compromised device, browser or extension.
  • You trust the code this site serves. Security is never stronger than that and than the channel you used for the codes.

Browsers

Current desktop Chrome, Edge, Firefox and Safari, and Safari on iPhone and iPad (also as an app added to the Home Screen). Android Chrome should work but is not a primary target yet.

Under the hood

Rust compiled to WebAssembly owns all protocol state. WebRTC DataChannel for transport, public STUN only for address discovery, Noise KK (X25519, ChaCha20-Poly1305, BLAKE2s) pinned to the exchanged codes, a 6-digit + emoji safety code. The full design, including its limits, is in the specification.
